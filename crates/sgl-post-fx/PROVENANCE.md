# Provenance and algorithm notes

The initial Rust/WGSL implementation was ported from DiligentFX revision
`f26cfe5b901bf180c4a3c9bbd4d5df0b96536d4b` and
DiligentCore revision `18bfa7b7563a0ef5b5fe074d37c2e8304100e965`
(`HLSLDefinitions.fxh`), retained unedited in `vendor/`.

`sgl-post-fx` is now an SGL-owned effects library, evolving under
[RD-2](../../specs/sgl3d.md#rendering-development). Upstream equivalence and
feature parity are not requirements. Keep source attribution and licences,
and maintain notes when they explain an algorithm or implementation decision;
new changes do not require an exhaustive difference catalogue.

The notes below preserve the original port's adaptations and subsequent fixes.
The DFX identifiers remain stable because code and tests refer to them. They
are historical references, not an approval process or a claim that unlisted
code matches upstream.

| DFX-14 | TAA's `MotionFactor` (pixel speed) scales both the variance gamma and the history weight, so any pixel moving faster than 1/`TAA_MOTION_VECTOR_DIFF_FACTOR` (256) of the viewport per frame loses its history. That contradicts the constant's documentation ("the difference in motion vectors between the current frame and the previous frame"). `ClipToAABB` moves history along the line to the current colour until any one channel enters the variance box, extrapolating up to `TAA_VARIANCE_INTERSECTION_MAX_T` (10) times past the current colour. | History is rejected by the difference between the pixel's motion and the previous frame's closest motion at its reprojected position, gradually, as Godot's TAA rejects ("velocity disocclusion", `get_factor_disocclusion` in `servers/rendering/renderer_rd/shaders/effects/taa_resolve.glsl` with `servers/rendering/renderer_rd/effects/taa.cpp`, revision `b13043816a0f234985030ec035363a005bc86c32`, MIT, `LICENSE-godot.txt`; the shader is based on Spartan Engine's TAA, MIT, `LICENSE-spartan.txt`): none within 2.5 pixels (`taa.cpp`'s `disocclusion_threshold`; the shader's comment of 0.1/max(resolution) is stale), then a share of 0.01 per pixel. Godot adds that share to its 1/16 current weight (`RPC_16`), so the history weight is at most the cap less the share (`TemporalStabilityFactor`, 0.9375, Godot's 1 - 1/16; DFX-19's 0.985 at still pixels), and none is left 93.75 pixels beyond the threshold. DiligentFX's accumulated confidence still ramps the weight up after a reset or disocclusion, but is not multiplied by the share: through `ComputeCorrectedAlpha`'s 1/(2 - weight) that compounds frame over frame and settles at 1 - sqrt(share) (0.50 at 27.5 pixels, where Godot keeps 0.69). History is clipped towards the neighbourhood mean (Godot's `clip_aabb`, after Playdead) within a box of Godot's `variance_dynamic` standard deviations that narrows smoothly to none at 2% of the screen per frame (`clip_history_3x3`), so fast motion resolves towards the neighbourhood mean instead of the aliased current sample. `variance_dynamic` is 1.1 × 1080 / height clamped to 0.75–1; 1.1 at 1080 rows clamps to 1, so at rest the box is clamp(1188 / height, 0.75, 1) deviations: 1 up to 1188 rows, 0.75 from 1584. The deviations are of DiligentFX's tone-mapped colour (`HDRToSDR`), in YCoCg under `FeatureFlags::YCOCG_COLOR_SPACE` as Bevy measures its box, otherwise in RGB (SGL3D's Hydrogent flags); Godot measures linear HDR RGB. DiligentFX's clip extrapolated bright edges into coloured specks in such narrow boxes. The box applies to every pixel, DFX-19's still ones included: under the 4-pixel limit's rejection, a still pixel an object had just left lost its history; with the gradual rejection, DiligentFX's 2.5-deviation still box kept the object's trail. The resolve writes each frame's closest motion beside its accumulated frame, alternating with the accumulated buffers, and the next frame binds it as `g_TexturePrevMotion` (until #16, a `CopyTextureColor` of the context's after each execution). A frame that copies its input instead (DFX-2's first frame of a feature set) copies the motion vectors in their place, so the next frame compares with them: they differ from the closest motion only within a pixel of a depth edge. Owner decision 2026-09-28; Godot's gradual rejection, box and clip 2026-10-05 (#93). |
| DFX-15 | `ScreenXYDepthToViewSpace` (`PostFX_Common.fxh`) divides NDC by the projection's scale only and drops its off-centre terms, while `ProjectPosition` applies the whole matrix. Hydrogent passes the TAA-jittered projection (`TemporalAntiAliasing::GetJitteredProjMatrix` adds the jitter to `m20` and `m21`). SSR's screen-space ray therefore bends by the jitter scaled by its length, and the hit reads radiance texels away from the reflected point, differently each frame. | The reconstruction subtracts `m20` and `m21` from NDC first, the exact inverse of the projection in x and y. AMD's SSSR reconstructs the same way with its full inverse projection (`FFX_SSSR_ScreenSpaceToViewSpace`). Upstream bug fix, 2026-09-28. |
| DFX-16 | `SSR_ComputeSpatialReconstruction` averages raw radiance. The pass is Wicked Engine's `ssr_resolveCS` (the same BRDF-over-PDF ray reuse, weighted variance and ray-length reprojection depth) without its tone mapping, so one ray that hits an emitter dominates a glossy pixel's average. With `TemporalRadianceStabilityFactor` 1.0, the temporal pass clamps history to a neighbourhood box that such outliers widen and narrow. A static glossy reflection of bright emitters then sparkles, and its mean falls several times below the resolved radiance. | Samples are tone-mapped by 1/(1 + luminance) before the weighted average and variance, and the average is inverse tone-mapped, as Wicked's `ssr_resolveCS` does. The weight applies to confidence as well as colour, so the premultiplied radiance of DFX-17 stays consistent: the round trip is a luminance-weighted average of whole samples. A receiver whose reconstruction radius is under half a texel (perceptual roughness under 0.025) resolves only its own ray, which the round trip leaves unchanged. Fixes the static SSR sparkle the owner reported, 2026-09-28. |
| DFX-17 | `SSR_ComputeIntersection` stores a hit's full radiance whenever its confidence is above zero, and black for a miss, with the confidence in alpha. The spatial pass averages rays, so the colour carries the share of rays that hit but not their partial confidence. Hydrogent then composites `lerp(IBL, SSR, alpha)`, as Wicked does. That scales hits by the hit share twice and weak hits not at all, so reflected light fades too early and too dark wherever only some of a pixel's rays hit. | Each ray stores its radiance premultiplied by its confidence, so every average keeps colour and confidence consistent, and the transition fade (`AlphaInterpolation`) scales both. A consumer composites `radiance + (1 - confidence) * environment`, as Bevy composites its SSR, and as AMD's SSSR blends the environment into each missed ray. Owner decision 2026-09-28. |
| DFX-18 | `HierarchicalRaymarch` (`SSR_ComputeIntersection`, AMD's FidelityFX SSSR traversal) treats the depth buffer as infinitely thick: a ray ends at the first surface it passes behind. `ValidateHit` then rejects a hit more than `DepthBufferThickness` behind that surface, so every ray that passes behind a thin foreground object (a pole, a vehicle) on its way to a farther reflected surface falls back to the environment. | At the most detailed mip, a ray behind the surface by at least `DepthBufferThickness` (measured as `ValidateHit` measures it, where its confidence reaches zero) advances to the next cell at the same mip and continues, as Godot's hierarchical SSR does with its depth tolerance (`servers/rendering/renderer_rd/shaders/effects/screen_space_reflection.glsl`, revision `d851ae838de54a0bd3dde9043912f39c74265cdd`, MIT, `LICENSE-godot.txt`). | DFX-19 | `TAA_ComputeTemporalAccumulation` keeps at most `TemporalStabilityFactor` (0.9375) of history and rejects it by depth disocclusion at every pixel. The depth buffers it compares are rendered with different jitter, so with the camera still, sub-pixel geometry (distant light strips) changes depth between frames: its history is rejected every few frames and it blinks. The remaining 1/16 current weight leaves jittered detail and reflections shimmering in a still image. | A pixel whose closest motion is under 0.01 pixels on both axes keeps up to 0.985 of history and skips the depth test, as Bevy's TAA does for pixels that are not moving (`crates/bevy_anti_alias/src/taa/taa.wesl`, revision `92a29e701a6b0bf8846484c3999c2ba97d90dd06`, MIT, `LICENSE-bevy.txt`: `MIN_HISTORY_BLEND_RATE` 0.015, no depth rejection). Moving pixels are unchanged. Owner request 2026-09-29 (visible jitter while stopped). Since #93, still pixels take DFX-14's box instead of upstream's 2.5 deviations: at rest clamp(1188 / height, 0.75, 1) deviations clipped towards the neighbourhood mean, Bevy's 1σ clip (`taa.wesl` `clip_towards_aabb_center`) up to 1188 rows and tighter above (#94). |
| DFX-20 | `SampleReflectionVector` (`SSR_ComputeIntersection`) moves the second random number to 0 by `GGXImportanceSampleBias`. Under the spherical-cap VNDF sampler (`SmithGGXSampleVisibleNormalSC`, Dupuy and Benyoub 2023) that is the cap's pole, so at bias 1 the micro-normal is the normalised sum of the roughness-stretched view direction and the normal. It tilts towards the viewer by an angle that grows with roughness and towards grazing view, so rays rise above the mirror direction: at perceptual roughness 0.125 by about a fifth of their elevation 2° above the surface. Reflections far along a glossy floor are squashed towards what they reflect and stretch back as the camera approaches. | At bias 1 the micro-normal is the macro normal, so the ray follows the mirror direction, the lobe's peak, as Godot's SSR traces `reflect(view_dir, normal)` (`servers/rendering/renderer_rd/shaders/effects/screen_space_reflection.glsl`, revision `ed1daf0bf001b61586d9930840f2f1394092c079`, 4.7.2-stable, MIT, `LICENSE-godot.txt`); its PDF is evaluated there. Bias below 1 is unchanged. Owner request 2026-09-29 (distant reflections warping as the camera approaches). |
| DFX-21 | `SSR_ComputeStencilMaskAndExtractRoughness` discards rejected pixels before exporting roughness, although the half-resolution mask reads all pixels in a block. | DFX-23 | `ComputeSpatialReconstruction` preserves its render targets outside the reflection mask; `ComputePixelStatistic` in temporal accumulation reads every member of the current 3×3 neighbourhood. AMD FidelityFX SSSR's resolved-radiance analogue is its [Prefilter output](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-Denoiser/blob/d7dfecbabe7b9523b14e7b067216e06b86e8d189/ffx-reflection-dnsr/ffx_denoiser_reflections_prefilter.h) (the denoiser revision pinned by SSSR `34dcacd1feefcfab2855b82e76c7d711f2020a75`): the denoiser writes every pixel in a dispatched tile, copying current intersection radiance for non-reflective pixels. | DFX-24 | `ScreenSpaceReflection::m_FrameTimer` (Diligent's `Timer`) reads the system clock for the transition alpha's elapsed time, restarted while pipelines are pending. | The caller passes `RenderAttributes::frame_time`, the seconds since the previous execution, and the elapsed time is their sum since the restart, 0 at the first execution. | DFX-25 | SSR's temporal accumulation reprojects history twice, by the surface's motion and by the reflection's virtual point (the hit distance beyond the surface, SEED's dual reprojection), and keeps whichever history's luminance is nearer the current neighbourhood mean, clamped to 2.5 standard deviations. `ComputeResolvedDepth` (`SSR_ComputeSpatialReconstruction`) takes the camera-to-surface distance plus the hit distance as the virtual point's camera-space Z, which places it too far by the inverse cosine of its angle from the view axis, so the hit reprojection under-moves reflections. On glossy floors in fast motion, reflections of bright fixtures smear into streaks over 2–8 frames (#80: Crystal's history against none, beta 0.017; the hit reprojection alone smeared more, 0.023 at 60 Hz). | The virtual point lies the hit distance beyond the surface along the view ray, as AMD's reflection denoiser places it (`FFX_DNSR_Reflections_GetHitPositionReprojection`). A surface history whose squared distance from the neighbourhood mean reaches 1.5 times the length of its variance is rejected rather than clamped (`FFX_DNSR_REFLECTIONS_REPROJECT_SURFACE_DISCARD_VARIANCE_WEIGHT`, `FFX_DNSR_Reflections_PickReprojection`): the reflection moved by its own parallax, not with the surface. Both are from `ffx-reflection-dnsr/ffx_denoiser_reflections_reproject.h`, revision `d7dfecbabe7b9523b14e7b067216e06b86e8d189`, MIT, `LICENSE-amd-fidelityfx-denoiser.txt`. The discard differs from AMD's in two ways. AMD measures the neighbourhood over a radius-4 Gaussian-weighted window of raw intersection radiance, with the variance's absolute value; here it is the pass's own 3×3 box over spatially reconstructed radiance. AMD tests the surface history only after the hit candidate fails its normal and roughness test, so ties go to the hit; here the test applies whenever the luminance comparison prefers the surface candidate. The box clamp is Wicked Engine's 2 standard deviations (`ssr_temporalCS` `temporalScale`), whose 0.95 history weight the pass already keeps. The luminance choice between the candidates stays; AMD chooses by history normals, which this pass does not keep. Playdead's luminance-difference feedback measured alike but acts on every pixel, still ones included, and raises the history weight to 0.97, so it is not used. On Hyperdrive's route the smear falls to beta 0.009 at 30 and 60 Hz (the hit reprojection alone to 0.014). Still reflections measured unchanged (stationary floor flicker 0.084 before, 0.085 after): with the camera still both candidates are the same history, though the 2-deviation box applies to them too. A test (`resolved_depth_is_the_virtual_point_along_the_view_ray`) checks the resolved depth against the virtual point built and projected independently; upstream's formula misses it by about 30 % in camera depth 41° off axis. #92, 2026-10-05. |
| DFX-26 | `HierarchicalRaymarch` (`SSR_ComputeIntersection`) and AMD FidelityFX SSSR's core loop keep a ray that has reached the far plane in the loop: the hierarchy there holds the background, so the ray cannot advance and descends every mip before it ends, about 6 of a sky ray's 30 steps. | Stop a ray whose depth is within 1e-6 of the far plane, as [AMD's hybrid traversal](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/c6efa6bf7f2027b3ec94f28578bb5965eabb9e55/samples/hybridreflections/shaders/ffx_sssr_intersect.h#L141-L145) does. It ends as a miss, as before. A ray at the far plane behind geometry ends there rather than passing behind the geometry (DFX-18) to the screen edge. Its shorter length can change spatial reconstruction's weights by one 8-bit step at a few pixels. AMD's other early exit, `MinTraversalOccupancy` (ending a wave's rays once few are still marching, `ffx_sssr_intersect.h:195-200`), was measured with subgroup ballots on an Apple M5 and is not taken: it made the intersection 5% slower (#125). |
| DFX-27 | DiligentFX traces one ray per pixel at full resolution, and one per 2×2 block under `SSR_OPTION_HALF_RESOLUTION`. AMD FidelityFX SSSR's `ClassifyTiles` gives each mirror pixel a ray and each 2×2 quad of glossy pixels one, copied across the quad, plus a ray at any pixel whose variance history exceeds `varianceThreshold`, 0 in AMD's sample ([`ffx_sssr_classify_tiles.h:25-63`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/c6efa6bf7f2027b3ec94f28578bb5965eabb9e55/sdk/include/FidelityFX/gpu/sssr/ffx_sssr_classify_tiles.h#L25-L63), [`sssrrendermodule.h:78-81`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/c6efa6bf7f2027b3ec94f28578bb5965eabb9e55/samples/sssr/sssrrendermodule.h#L78-L81)). | Not taken (#125): Full keeps a ray per pixel and Half stays the cheaper tier. AMD's budget saves work through a compacted compute ray list with indirect dispatch; Crystal's intersection is DiligentFX's full-screen fragment pass. At AMD's threshold compaction would not help: 99% of the glossy pixels the rule would copy to have variance in the water and `ssr` examples, so it traces 99% of Full's rays. In the fragment pass, skipping the copied pixels saved nothing on an Apple M5 (water at 1920×1080 1.72 → 1.72 ms, `ssr` example 0.37 → 0.37 ms) and added 12–19% to spatial reconstruction: the pass's SIMD groups still run until their slowest ray. |
| DFX-28 | DiligentFX's and AMD FidelityFX SSSR's traversals keep a ray that has risen above every surface on the screen in the loop, crossing background cells at the top mip, until it leaves the screen (DFX-22) or reaches the far plane (DFX-26). | Not taken (#125): a sky envelope, beyond the engines, was measured and not adopted. A pass after the depth hierarchy recorded, for each column of its top mip, the first row holding a surface; a rising ray ended as a miss once it was above that row in every column left on its path. Misses were unchanged and rays took fewer steps (water 30.4 → 27.1, a mixed scene 30.4 → 28.2, `ssr` example 26.3 → 24.8; a per-pixel envelope reaches about −15%), since most of a sky ray's steps climb out of the reflecting surface's own cells. | DFX-29 | `ScreenSpaceReflection` runs spatial reconstruction, temporal accumulation and bilateral cleanup over every pixel of the reflection mask, including regions where every ray missed, which denoise to zero. AMD FidelityFX SSSR's `ClassifyTiles` lists the 8×8 tiles that hold a glossy reflective pixel, and its denoiser runs only over that list ([`ffx_sssr_classify_tiles.h:69-118`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/c6efa6bf7f2027b3ec94f28578bb5965eabb9e55/sdk/include/FidelityFX/gpu/sssr/ffx_sssr_classify_tiles.h#L69-L118), [`ffx_denoiser_reflections_reproject.h:713`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/c6efa6bf7f2027b3ec94f28578bb5965eabb9e55/sdk/include/FidelityFX/gpu/denoiser/ffx_denoiser_reflections_reproject.h#L713)); unlisted tiles keep whatever history they last had, zeroed only on the first frame ([`ffx_sssr.cpp:601-622`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/c6efa6bf7f2027b3ec94f28578bb5965eabb9e55/sdk/src/components/sssr/ffx_sssr.cpp#L601-L622)). | The three passes skip the 8×8 tiles where no ray within reach found a confident hit, beyond AMD's list, which skips only tiles without reflective pixels (#125). `SSR_ComputeDenoiserTiles`, SGL's own module kept apart as AMD keeps `ClassifyTiles`, runs two small passes after the intersection: one gathers the rays' confidence per 4×4 block of pixels, one marks each tile with a hit within reach; the three passes return at once elsewhere. The reach, conservative and along each axis, follows the settings: reconstruction reads rays up to `SpatialReconstructionRadius` away plus a pixel of rounding (two at half resolution, where a ray covers two pixels and rounds towards +x/+y), the temporal neighbourhood adds a pixel, and the bilateral kernel `min(2σ, 2)` truncated; a tile is active when a hit lies within that many tiles, rounded up. No radiance changes at any settings. Unlike AMD's unlisted tiles, a skipped tile's histories are written. Its radiance history holds zero, the value the history clamp gives there, so no older reflection returns with new hits. Its variance history holds 1, DiligentFX's own value where a pixel has no history (`SSR_ComputeTemporalAccumulation.fx:260`): a reflection returning there is treated as disoccluded, and as its variance decays by `TemporalVarianceStabilityFactor` (0.9) to bilateral cleanup's 0.001 estimate threshold, about 65 frames, cleanup blurs it over 3×3 pixels where its perceptual roughness is 1/16 or more. On an Apple M5 the three passes, the 0.06 ms of finding the tiles included, take 1.22 → 0.60 ms on a lake at 1920×1080, 1.62 → 0.66 ms on a scene of mixed ray lengths (Full and Half alike) and 0.41 → 0.17 ms in the `ssr` example at 960×540. The final frames differ by at most 13/255 at 9 pixels of the lake (variance 0 instead of 1 left 67 pixels over 8/255). |

A corrupt or careless attribute can keep the GPU busy long enough to hang it. | Each loop has a fixed bound (AR-12 in `specs/sgl3d-architecture.md`), owned by `ScreenSpaceReflectionStructures`: lookups at most `SSR_MAX_TRAVERSAL_INTERSECTIONS` (256) and the reconstruction radius at most `SSR_SPATIAL_RECONSTRUCTION_MAX_RADIUS` (8), the tops of DiligentFX's own ranges for them (`ScreenSpaceReflection::UpdateUI`, `ScreenSpaceReflection.cpp:389` and `:423`), the radius clamped where reconstruction reads it too so the tiles' reach still covers it; and the bilateral kernel's radius at most `SSR_BILATERAL_MAX_RADIUS` (2), its `Radius`'s largest, whose literal it replaces. Attributes within those ranges are unchanged (#160). |
