# Audit log

What each audit and architecture pass found, what was done about it and what
now keeps it from coming back. One terse entry per finding: the defect, its
cause, the fix and why that fix, and its guard (a spec clause, a test or a
scenario). Work is tracked in the linked issues; this file is the history that
outlives them. The [architect skill](../.claude/skills/sgl-architect/SKILL.md)
appends a section for every pass.

Entries are never rewritten once their issue is closed. A later finding about
the same code gets its own entry that refers back.

## 2026-11-17 — rendering audit

Commit `941ec7bd`. Scope: every SGL3D rendering feature and `sgl-post-fx`,
read against the specs and against the engines each was ported from, at the
revisions the ports' headers name. Nine passes: temporal antialiasing; FSR2
and FSR3.1 integration; Crystal SSR and the post-effect context; Velvet,
world-space reflections, the ray scene and probes; shadows; ambient occlusion
and diffuse GI; exposure, bloom, grading, tone mapping, SMAA, FSR1 and motion
blur; volumetric fog and the transparent stage; the shading model, lights,
clusters, decals and GPU culling. Every finding below was confirmed against the
code before it was filed.

the scenario harness or while fixing, and are kept here because they are the

### Records that were wrong

The code was right. Corrected with this log unless noted.

- The README's temporal antialiasing section predated #767 and did not describe the motion-adaptive history; corrected by A-1's fix, not here.
- The Shared contracts' statement that every history "rejects what changed by reprojection and clamping" no longer held for TAA at rest, where a shading-only change converged over tens of frames; A-23 amended it.
- `settings.md` said ambient occlusion runs at scene resolution; it runs at render resolution.
- `AutoExposure`'s documentation still described Bevy's metering of near-black samples, which D-46 replaced.
- The README's receiver limits omitted `keeps_specular` and transmissive receivers; the README and `material.rs` said the unrefracted transmission fallback keeps its tint, where it passes the mean.
- The probe influence ramp was attributed to Godot, whose ramp is squared and clamped to the box.
- FSR1's RCAS comment called 0.2 stops "the FSR sample's 0.8 sharpness"; the SDK maps 0.8 to 0.4 stops, and 0.2 stops is Godot's default.
- The Shared contracts' determination of indirect diffuse light, its Bevy precedent, the README and the comment above `surface_indirect_diffuse` read as if a baked chart or an ambient cube replaced the frame's ambient; the code adds them. The owner decided additive (D-56), and the records now say so; corrected by [#779](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/779), not here. Guard: a lightmapped and a moving square's lit colour against values computed by hand.

### Guards that were missing

Invariants the code relied on that no spec clause or test protected. Each is
now stated in `sgl3d-architecture.md`; what tests it is said beside it.

- FSR's `motionVectorScale` is minus the render size because motion is current minus previous UV; the raster jitter is the clip-space translation of the pixel offset reported to the resolver (the Temporal design). Tested: the FSR scenarios fail on a wrong sign, and the jitter case holds it sub-pixel and out of the motion.
- The motion target's UV is texture space, +y down (the Temporal design). Tested: the suite's motion vectors against the script.
- Cascade map sizes are powers of two, which the texel snapping relies on (the Shadows design). Tested at every `ShadowQuality` size.
- Ambient occlusion runs only under a centred reversed-Z perspective projection (Frame item 5). Stated; no test.
- The fog volume is indexed by planar view depth, never distance along the ray (the Fog design). Stated; the one blended-fog test reads the centre pixel, where the two agree.
- Cluster lookups pass render-size pixels and the clustered view's depth (Shared contracts, Lights and shadows). Stated; no test.

### Waiting on the owner

- Whether to report the ordering gap behind A-26 to wgpu. SGL works around it. Decided: neither a report nor a fork. A wgpu-only reproduction shows it on wgpu 30.0.1, 30.0.2 and trunk, so the bug is wgpu's; upstream already tracks it as [gfx-rs/wgpu#9215](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/issues/9215), and draft [gfx-rs/wgpu#10292](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu/pull/10292) fixes every case the reproduction covers. The workaround measured clean, and the reproduction, its results and SGL's remaining exposure (a BLAS built and traced in one command buffer, reported upstream on M3 and iPhone, never seen on the M5) are on [#817](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/817).

### Left as they are

Design choices and open ends, recorded so a later pass does not rediscover them.

- At rest, motion-adaptive TAA's slower feedback delays shading-only changes that mark no mask (a light switching, a flash). Filament has the same trade-off.
- The directional light's disc sampling is slightly centre-weighted for a large `angular_diameter`, as Wicked Engine's is.
- TAA frames write FSR's two mask targets. One target was measured to recover at most 0.16 ms of the blended pass at 1920x1080, for six more blended pipelines ([#812](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/812)).
- A responsive mask's hold stays at full strength for all its frames, where the other holds ramp down: a ramp left partly covered edge pixels short of the departing bar (A-23).
- The categorical sky's arriving half has no scenario: an arriving surface's own motion re-arms the hold first ([#816](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/816)).
- A world-reflection texel is judged by its block's first pixel while its ray is cast from the jittered one, as Wicked's is; measured within one rounding step at every phase ([#814](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/814)).
- A one-pixel slit showing a far surface, between bars wider than the upsample's search reaches, keeps its neighbours' ray-traced shadow (A-25).
- The largest finite float is a literal in five shaders: a constant of the number format, not a formula SGL owns.
- Open: FSR3 converges on sub-pixel strips more slowly than its sampling allows in its first jitter cycle at Quality and Balanced, possibly AMD's accumulation; its bar is parked on `scenario/issue-810` ([#810](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/810)).

## 2026-11-17 — web target pass

Commit `ca3c65c5`. Scope: every native-only path in the library crates, read
against D-48 (the web supports everything its platform can run): each
`target_arch` gate outside tests, each optional device feature SGL or its FSR
port requests, and each platform exception the guides record. WebGPU's
capabilities are from the specification's feature index (editor's draft of this
date) and wgpu 30.0.1's WebGPU backend (`FEATURES_MAPPING`); browser support is
from MDN's compatibility data. [#782](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/782).

### Native-only that the web could run

- **FSR2 and FSR3.** Of the five device features the port requires, the platform lacks one: texture atomics, for which atomics in a storage buffer are an exact substitute (minimum and maximum do not depend on order). Subgroups, 16-bit normalized formats and the storage formats are in WebGPU (`subgroups`, `texture-formats-tier1`, `texture-formats-tier2`) and missing only from wgpu's browser backend; `float32-filterable` is granted. Planned in [#803](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/803).
- **Blocking readbacks.** `diagnostics::read`, `Renderer::capture_specular_probe` and `sgl_2d`'s `read_scene` are native-only only because they block; WebGPU maps asynchronously, and the browser example already carries its own awaiting copy of the first. Planned in [#804](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/804).
- **Blocking readbacks, closed** by [#804](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/804): each is now an asynchronous function, its one implementation, with the blocking name a native wrapper (`diagnostics::read_async`, `Renderer::capture_specular_probe_async`, `read_scene_async`); the browser lane reads its frames through the first and captures a probe through the second.

### Records that were wrong

- The feature guide said FSR needs "native-only device features" without saying which or why, and the README's list omitted subgroups. Both now give the reason, split by whether the platform or wgpu's backend is the obstacle.
- The feature guide gave no reason for hardware ray tracing's absence in the browser (WebGPU has no ray queries).
- `sgl-net`'s README did not say what a browser peer loses by having only the WebSocket.

### Waiting on the owner

- Whether browser peers get unreliable datagrams. WebTransport's are in every major browser and fit `DatagramTransport` as it is; the cost is a QUIC and HTTP/3 server in-process. Decided: wanted, by WebTransport, and built when a game needs it; it stays open as planned work ([#805](https://github.com/stevepryde/sgl/issues/805)).

### Left as they are

- Hardware ray tracing: WebGPU has neither acceleration structures nor ray queries. Rays trace the software BVHs, as on any device without them.
- `asset::load` and `capture_scene` take a path: a browser has no file system. `load_slice` and `sgl-2d`'s `fetch_runtime_bundle` are the browser's ways in.
- The 1 GiB default load budget and the 2 GiB decal atlas build limit on `wasm32`: its 4 GiB address space, shared with the game's other content.
- The blocking constructors (`Gpu::headless`, `Context::new`, `try_new`) are native conveniences over asynchronous forms the browser awaits.
- The other way round, nothing was found: every optional WebGPU feature SGL3D uses natively it also requests in the browser (depth clip control, RG11B10 render targets, BC, ETC2 and ASTC, timestamp queries), and the WebGPU features it leaves unused (dual-source blending, clip distances, indirect first instance, blendable 32-bit float) it does not use natively either.

## 2026-11-18 — temporal history audit

Commit `d3b785f2`. Scope: every condition under which anything in SGL3D or
`sgl-post-fx` discards temporal history, weakens it, withholds jitter,
restarts a stage, or zeroes, replaces or disregards a motion vector, with the
AMD shaders SGL3D ships through `sp-fidelity` 0.3.2 read as SGL3D's own
behaviour. Five readings: the frame, the renderer and motion vectors;
temporal antialiasing; FSR2 and FSR3; reflections; shadows, ambient
occlusion, fog, dynamic GI, exposure and the remaining stages. Asked for by
the owner after a run of artefacts that each traced to a rejection rule
nobody had written down. The audit registered 224 conditions; each is now an
entry of [SGL3D temporal history](sgl3d-history.md), which is a rule, a
defect or an open question, and that register's first section says how it is
kept. No issue was filed for the defects below: each was fixed within the
audit, or kept by measurement as a rule (B-20).

### Defects

Identifiers are the register's. Two were confirmed in a game before the
reading: with the craft at rest, Hyperdrive's capture harness at 1920x1080
showed thin emitters flickering under TAA while B-1's signal was raised in
400 of 400 frames (the city's traffic), and ignoring it cut the pixels that
changed in more than a quarter of frames from 13,292 to 2,637; with the game
running and the craft at rest, the camera's view matrix differed from the
last frame's by 2e-6 to 2e-5 in 80 of 100 frames (B-2), and ignoring B-1
alone then changed nothing.

| # | Finding | Cause | Fix, and what guards it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B-1 | TAA never reaches resting stability in a scene where any rigid instance moves, anywhere. | The view-wide signal Filament raises for visible renderables that moved was ported as "any visible-flagged instance whose pose changed", before the motion reach existed; a moving instance writes motion that the per-pixel hold, the closest-motion search and the reach already follow. | A pose change of a `Mobility::Moving` instance whose last submitted pose applies (visible then and now, the same model) no longer raises the scene's signal: it writes motion, which arms the pixels it covers and leaves through the pixel's own motion, the closest-motion search and the reach, as deformation always has; every change without a motion vector (appearance, departure, showing or hiding, a model swap, a static instance moved, a mover with no applicable pose, geometry, drawing-changing material edits, the visibility mask) still raises it. Narrowing the signal rather than gating it by screen or distance follows the reason it exists: only what has no motion vector needs it. Movers faster than the reach now share deformation's limit (T-23). Guard: the scene's signal rule (`rigid_recovery_leaves_moves_with_motion_to_the_pixels`), resting stability beside a rigid mover in view and behind the camera, and no ghost where a card or a 2-pixel strip moved by its pose at 0.5 to 16 px a frame (`a_rigid_card_moves_from_rest`), with case 9 and a moved static instance still re-arming. |
| B-2 | A camera at rest tens of metres from the render origin re-arms TAA across the view on most frames. | Filament's 1e-6 is applied to double-precision matrices on the CPU; the port compares `f32` products on the GPU, where 1e-6 is one rounding step at a magnitude of 16. | Camera motion is decided on the CPU (`TemporalAntiAliasing::camera_moved`, passed to the resolve as `camera_motion`) from the unjittered views and projections the caller supplied, element by element and never as products, agreeing within eight rounding steps of each element's magnitude, and for the view's translation of the eye's distance from the origin, since a translation element is a cancelling sum of terms of the eye's size (a first version judged it by its own magnitude, and Hyperdrive's idle chase camera 100 m out still moved in 229 of 300 frames on an element of 0.024 changing by 7.6e-6 to 1.5e-5); so rest is rest at any distance from the origin, as Filament's double-precision comparison intends. Eight steps covers a view recomputed from float state, and is safe because a camera change that moves any pixel by 0.01 px still arms that pixel through its motion. ### Waiting on the owner

- O-1: whether one renderer may draw more than one view a frame, and what two renderers of one scene see. Decided by the owner (D-57) and implemented: `Renderer` holds what every view of a scene shares, `View` one camera's frame and histories, and `Renderer::render(scene, &mut [ViewFrame])` is one frame of the scene, `views[0]` its primary; `finish_frame(scene)` names no views (each view settles its render by its own frame attempt's flag, which `finish_frame` sets), and a view's history continues only from the scene's last finished frame (F-1, F-12, both rules). Two renderers drawing one scene in one frame are not supported: the second's `render` supersedes the first's attempt, and the first's views reset every frame (F-12). Its third part, edits between `render` and `finish_frame`, is settled as F-13: the scene records what a frame draws when `render` begins it (`Scene::begin_frame`) and commits that record at `finish_frame`, so an edit after the submission belongs to the next frame, as Wicked 4323a33 and Bevy 9d12036 capture "previous" at the frame's scene step. Guards: `views_match_their_solo_runs` (TAA, FSR2, FSR3; every view bit-identical to the view rendered alone), `views_match_their_solo_runs_with` (Crystal, Velvet, world rays, ambient occlusion, fog, dynamic GI), `a_view_keeps_its_history_while_another_appears_resizes_and_leaves`, `a_view_that_skips_a_frame_restarts`, `shared_work_runs_once_a_frame`, `scene_work_follows_any_view_that_traces`, `each_view_draws_only_its_own_shadow_faces`, `edits_after_submission_belong_to_the_next_frame` (`tests/sgl-3d/tests/scenario_views.rs`); `finish_frame_of_another_scene_commits_nothing`, `a_view_used_between_render_and_finish_keeps_its_history`, `a_view_of_another_renderer_is_refused` (`renderer/view_tests.rs`); `a_frame_commits_what_it_drew`, `an_unfinished_frame_commits_nothing` (`scene/content_tests.rs`).
- B-20: whether to keep Crystal's surface-history discard as measured (R-9). Kept by default: it removes 16–17 % of the fast-motion smear for about 5 % more flicker under slow camera motion. The alternatives are taking the hit history in place of the discard (removes the misfire, keeps 14–15 % of the smear reduction), or keeping normal and roughness history in Crystal so AMD's own hit test can be measured.
- B-11: whether a ray-traced shadow slot is reserved for a light that leaves the ranking for its own sake (a returning light kept in its tier). - O-5: whether blended receivers mark reactivity. It bounds any exact oracle over dynamic GI to a volume the budget does not saturate (the views scenarios use 18 probes). Found by the views work, not addressed here; whether to make admission deterministic is a decision.
- Known limits of several views a frame (D-57), no issue filed yet:
  - L1: rays see the scene under the primary view's visibility mask, which lives in the scene's ray source header, and a view's output cannot yet be a scene texture.
  - L2: each view compiles its own stage pipelines (a second `View::new` takes about 22 ms once a process has made one) and holds its own local-light shadow atlases (128 MiB a view at High, 32 MiB at Low).
  - L3: FSR restarts after an unfinished frame (D-58, U-2).

### Left as they are

- AMD's previous-depth reconstruction writes each pixel's current depth at its previous position, so scenery now nearer than a followed surface that was behind it a frame ago flags that surface disoccluded (U-17, U-40). It is the algorithm, with correct inputs; scenario case U9 holds the inputs and places its scenery where the reconstruction is exact.
- FSR's velocity-based history weights (U-28, U-47), its shading-change and lock rules (U-31, U-43, U-44): AMD's, listed with what a game sees.
- The motion reach's limits (M-14, T-23): surfaces within a tile of motion of a pixel a frame lose resting stability while it moves, by design.
- Surfaces under blended materials, glow, mist and heat distortion are marked every frame and stay at the strength their mask gives (M-11, M-17a to M-17c).
